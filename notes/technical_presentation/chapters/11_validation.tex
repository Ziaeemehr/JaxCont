\begin{alertblock}{Higher levels supplement lower levels}
Agreement with another package does not excuse a failed residual; a small residual does not prove that the branch, event label, or scientific interpretation is correct.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Read diagnostics before declaring success or failure}
\scriptsize
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.27}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.24\textwidth}
                  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.31\textwidth}
                  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{.37\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Observation & What it can mean & Next action\\
\midrule
non-convergence, large residual, invalid points, early termination
  & no accepted numerical solution for the requested step
  & inspect the seed and method; reduce the step or revise tolerances with justification\\
answer moves under mesh, tolerance, step, or direction changes
  & discretization or branch-tracking sensitivity
  & refine and compare the repeated runs before interpreting events\\
shape agrees only after a shift, reorder, or rescale
  & conventions differ; this is not automatically a solver bug
  & align phase, units, state order, orientation, and interpolation first\\
one metric passes while another fails
  & partial agreement, not a global pass
  & report both: \texttt{MC-LC-002} has small event-location errors but misses LPC/PD labels and has critical-multiplier error $\approx1.10\times10^{-2}>5\times10^{-3}$\\
curves appear to overlap
  & a useful visual sanity check only
  & compute a declared metric against a declared tolerance\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Align conventions before comparing numbers}
\small
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Model and branch coordinates}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{state ordering} --- compare the same variables;
  \item \textbf{parameter units} --- convert before differencing;
  \item \textbf{branch orientation} --- reverse traversal if needed;
  \item \textbf{interpolation grid} --- compare on a common monotone segment.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\column{.48\textwidth}
\begin{block}{Cycle and spectrum coordinates}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{phase origin} --- remove circular shifts;
  \item \textbf{phase units} --- radians versus cycle fractions;
  \item \textbf{eigenvalue/multiplier matching} --- match uniquely, not by file order;
  \item \textbf{trivial multiplier removal} --- remove exactly one nearest $+1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
\end{columns}
\vspace{2mm}
\begin{alertblock}{Alignment is part of the method}
Changing conventions to minimize an error after seeing the answer is not validation; document the alignment rule and apply it reproducibly.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{alertblock}{Narrow claim}
The shooting example validates the adjoint/PRC computation on JaxCont-reconverged orbits. It does not independently validate orbit finding, the scientific model, global dynamics, or unsupported continuation families.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What to remember}
\large
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Start with run health, then repeat the computation before seeking an external oracle.
  \item Analytic, shooting, MatCont, and BifurcationKit comparisons become meaningful only after conventions are aligned reproducibly.
  \item A metric needs a declared tolerance; partial failures stay visible, and a convincing plot is not proof of correctness.
\end{enumerate}
